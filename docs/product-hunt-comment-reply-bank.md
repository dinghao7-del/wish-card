## Positioning

### If Someone Calls It A Chore App

```text
That is a fair first read, and it is one of the positioning risks I’m watching closely.

The chore/reward layer is only one part. The bigger loop is family profile -> AI routine -> kid-sized tasks -> stars -> real wishes -> parent promises -> weekly review. I want WishCard to help families run the whole rhythm, not only assign chores.
```

### If Someone Says It Feels Broad

```text
I agree that the scope can sound broad. That is why the PH demo focuses on one narrow loop: one parent, one child, one school week, one redeemed wish, and one weekly review.

The product can become larger over time, but the core promise has to stay simple: turn family plans into action kids can follow.
```

### If Someone Asks Why It Is A Family Operating System

```text
I use that phrase because WishCard connects planning, execution, motivation, parent follow-through, and review.

Most tools cover one layer: calendar, tasks, rewards, or notes. The interesting part here is whether those layers can become one repeatable family rhythm.
```

## AI Questions

### Why Use AI?

```text
AI is most useful at the blank-page moment.

Parents often know the goals but do not want to rebuild the schedule every week. WishCard uses family constraints such as age, school rhythm, parent availability, goals, and friction points to draft a realistic plan that parents can edit.
```

### Does AI Replace Parent Judgment?

```text
No. The parent stays in control.

The AI suggests a routine and explains its assumptions, but the parent approves, edits, or ignores it. The goal is to reduce planning load, not outsource parenting.
```

### Is The AI Output Live In The Demo?

```text
For the Product Hunt demo, the AI plan is deterministic so the launch experience stays reliable.

The real product direction is AI-assisted planning that parents can review and adjust before turning it into tasks.
```

## Parent And Child Trust

### Child Accounts

```text
Children do not need their own email or phone accounts.

The model is parent-controlled: kids can have family member profiles, while parents manage the account, data, plans, and promises.
```

### Ads

```text
No child-facing ads.

That is an important product boundary. If recommendations ever exist, they should be parent-side, transparent, and opt-in.
```

### Privacy

```text
Privacy is central because this product handles family routines and child motivation.

The launch position is parent control, no child email requirement, no child-facing ads, and anonymized community templates by default.
```

## Pricing

### What Will Be Free?

```text
The basic family loop should stay free: members, tasks, habits, stars, wishes, and a simple weekly review.

The launch is helping us learn which advanced planning layers parents actually value.
```

### What Would Be Paid?

```text
The likely paid layer is AI weekly planning, plan-to-task generation, deeper reports, multi-child planning, holiday planning, calendar sync, and expert templates.

I’m using this launch to test whether Plus or Pro has stronger demand before pushing payment too early.
```

### Why Subscription?

```text
If WishCard becomes part of a family’s weekly rhythm, subscription makes more sense than one-off purchase.

That said, I want the free loop to be genuinely useful before asking parents to pay.
```

## Comparisons

### Cozi Or Family Calendar Apps

```text
Cozi-style tools are strong for organizing family information.

WishCard focuses on what happens after planning: AI routine -> kid-sized tasks -> visible progress -> wish rewards -> parent promises -> weekly review.
```

### Kids Reward Apps

```text
Kids reward apps usually start from tasks and points.

WishCard starts earlier with family planning and ends later with weekly review and parent promise fulfillment. Rewards matter, but they sit inside a larger family rhythm.
```

### Habit Trackers

```text
Habit trackers are useful when the routine is already clear.

WishCard is aimed at families who need help shaping the routine first, then turning it into small actions a child can actually follow.
```

## Product Scope

### Schools And Teachers

```text
I’m starting with families because the daily execution loop happens at home.

Longer term, I can imagine teachers, coaches, or education creators sharing structured templates, but the core family workflow has to be strong first.
```

### Expert Templates

```text
Expert templates could be a strong Pro direction.

The important rule is that templates should adapt to the family context instead of becoming generic advice that parents still have to translate manually.
```

### Multi-Child Families

```text
Multi-child planning is one of the clearest Plus use cases.

The hard part is not creating more tasks. It is balancing different ages, energy levels, school rhythms, and parent availability without making the system feel heavy.
```
