## 2. Maker First Comment

Use this as the launch-day first comment:

```text
Hi Product Hunt,

I’m building WishCard because family life often breaks down not from one big problem, but from repeated planning, reminding, checking, and renegotiating every day.

WishCard is an AI family planner that turns a child’s profile and family constraints into weekly routines, then converts those routines into daily tasks kids can actually follow.

The part I care about most is the wish loop: kids earn stars, redeem real wishes, and parents get gentle reminders to fulfill those promises. The goal is not to gamify parenting for its own sake, but to help families build trust, rhythm, and positive motivation.

For this launch, I’d love feedback on three things:

1. Does the “AI family planner” positioning make sense?
2. Would the plan -> tasks -> stars -> wishes -> weekly review loop be useful in your family?
3. What would make this worth paying for: AI plans, weekly reviews, holiday planning, or expert templates?

Thank you for taking a look. I’ll be here all day responding to comments.
```

## 3. Common Product Hunt Replies

### If Someone Says It Looks Like A Chore App

```text
That is a fair first impression, and it is exactly the positioning challenge I’m trying to solve.

The chore/reward loop is only one layer. The bigger loop is family profile -> AI routine -> daily tasks -> stars -> real wishes -> parent promises -> weekly review. I want WishCard to help parents plan and adjust the whole family rhythm, not just assign chores.
```

### If Someone Asks Why AI Is Needed

```text
The AI part is useful when parents do not want to start from a blank page every week.

The goal is not to let AI parent the child. It is to turn family constraints such as age, school rhythm, parent availability, goals, and friction points into a realistic routine that parents can edit and approve.
```

### If Someone Mentions Child Privacy

```text
That is a core concern for this product.

The model is parent-controlled: children do not need email or phone accounts, child-facing ads are not part of the product, and community templates should be anonymized by default. Any future commercial recommendations should be parent-side, transparent, and opt-in.
```

### If Someone Asks About Pricing

```text
The basic family loop should stay free: members, tasks, habits, stars, wishes, and a simple review.

The paid layer would likely be AI weekly planning, plan-to-task generation, deeper reports, holiday planning, multi-child planning, and expert templates. This launch is partly to learn which of those parents actually value most.
```

### If Someone Asks About Schools Or Teachers

```text
I’m starting with families because the daily execution loop happens at home.

Longer term, I can imagine teachers, coaches, or education creators sharing structured routine templates, but I want the core family workflow to be strong before expanding into school or expert channels.
```

### If Someone Asks How This Differs From Cozi Or Family Calendar Apps

```text
Cozi-style tools are great for organizing family information.

WishCard is focused on the execution loop after planning: AI routine -> kid-sized tasks -> visible progress -> wish rewards -> parent promises -> weekly review. It is less about storing the calendar and more about helping the family follow through.
```

### If Someone Asks How This Differs From Kids Reward Apps

```text
Kids reward apps usually start from tasks and points.

WishCard starts earlier with family planning and ends later with weekly review and parent promise fulfillment. The reward loop matters, but it sits inside a larger family operating rhythm.
```

## 7. FAQ Short Answers

### Do children need their own accounts?

No. WishCard is designed around a parent-controlled family account. Children can use family member profiles without email or phone accounts.

### Does WishCard show ads to kids?

No. The child experience is not an ad surface. Any future commercial recommendation should be parent-side, transparent, and opt-in.

### Is AI making parenting decisions?

No. AI suggests plans from family constraints. Parents review, edit, approve, and stay in control.

### What makes WishCard different from a chore tracker?

WishCard starts before chores with family planning and continues after tasks with wishes, parent promises, and weekly reviews.

### What will be free?

The basic family loop should stay free: members, tasks, habits, stars, wishes, and simple weekly review.

### What might be paid?

AI weekly planning, plan-to-task generation, advanced reports, holiday planning, multi-child planning, community template adaptation, and expert templates.
